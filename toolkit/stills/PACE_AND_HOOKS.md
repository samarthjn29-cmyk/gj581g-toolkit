# Pace and hooks (Sam, 13 Nov 2026: "more gags ... feel faster without cutting length"; "use psychological tricks to keep our audience hooked")

Measured baseline (Aliens v2): 26-49 visual changes a minute; ch3/ch4 slowest. Pilot ch4 26 -> 42 changes/min, 8 -> 17 gags/min, same 99 s.

## Pictures and gags
- Target about 40 visual changes a minute and nothing new-free longer than about 3 s (visible_lint BARE).
- Rotate gag kinds: never the same kind twice in a row, and no kind more than about a third of a chapter's gags. Labels are the default filler; swap some for stamps, asides, chips, notify, lens, tag, counter, meter, loading, starburst, weather, circle, sticker.
- Punch-ins (move 'punch') on key words add energy without a new picture (2 s picture floor still holds).
- Thin anything abstract: a label must name something in the picture; ideas go to chips/asides without arrows.

## Music (toolkit: aliens/pace_bed.py)
- One Kevin MacLeod track per chapter (CC BY 4.0, credited), switching in the 1.2 s breath before each chapter; bouncier tracks for comic stretches, calmer at the end.
- Slide each track so its beats land on as many picture changes as possible (pilot: 8 of 24 vs ~4.7 by chance).
- Drop the music to silence under the biggest deadpan line in each chapter; it returns on the next picture.
- Bed about -29 LUFS (2 dB up from -31), still ducked under the voice.
- Research: faster, more arousing music makes time feel like it passes faster (Psychological Research 2023, doi 10.1007/s00426-023-01835-7), though it can make short intervals seem longer in lab estimates (Droit-Volet et al. 2013, Frontiers 10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00417): the effect comes from arousal plus change, not tempo alone.

## Hooks that keep viewers watching (apply in script, pictures and sound)
- Open loops: the opening question is restated by each chapter's title; plants flagged aloud and paid off by name ("Hold that thought", the 72 minutes).
- Running gags that escalate (NOTHING · 1, 2, 3, 4) reward attention and give viewers something to wait for.
- Pattern interrupts about every 20-40 s: a full-screen card, a drop-out, a punch-in, a picture style change.
- Contrast: loud/quiet, busy/bare, music/silence; the silence is what makes the rest feel fast.
- Curiosity before the answer: show the question (chips, "?" counters that resolve on the word: "SEEN AGAIN: ? of 8" -> 0).
- Chapter endings are buttons (a gag that lands), never a fade-out; the next chapter's title arrives after a short breath.
- Never write out a punchline before it is spoken; never put a figure on screen that isn't in the fact-checked script.
