# GJ 581g: thumbnail style library

The catalogue of thumbnail styles we can use, chosen per video by what the topic and the title need. Started 3 Nov 2026 from Sam's six reference thumbnails and a YouTube study of 55 high-performing thumbnails (15 searches: economics, prices, inflation, personal finance, how money works, history of money, business case studies, science animation, psychology, AI, behavioural economics, video essays, India's economy). References are kept as links only; no other creator's images are stored.

**How to use it:** at the angle stage, pick 2-3 styles that fit the angle (`decked-out-youtube-packaging`); in the full pass, make four thumbnails in four different styles, check them in the dark feed mock, and send three to YouTube's Test & compare. Record the winner's style ID in the post-publish review below, so the library learns what our audience clicks.

**Rules every style keeps:** one focal point; 2-4 words that add to the title, never repeat it; the video's palette, display face and series device; the bottom-right 140x60 px clear (duration badge); readable at 380 px and at about 170 px wide; no real logos or brands, no recognisable faces, no frames from other creators; numbers fact-checked or the video's own hypothetical. Talking-head styles (a presenter's face pointing at something) are out for us: our version puts a Folk character in that role.

### TS01 Editorial minimal
- **Look:** light paper ground, a heavy grotesk line with one italic serif word for contrast, a small data device (ruler, progress bar, tiny chart) and a hand-written note with an arrow.
- **Why it works:** calm in a loud feed; the device creates a "where am I on this?" question.
- **Seen in:** Sam's reference "From zero to $10K a month".

### TS02 Hero object on white
- **Look:** one real object photographed on white with its shadow (stock photo or cut-out), a torn accent banner with 2-4 words, at most one small tag.
- **Seen in:** Sam's references "Chinese Takeover" (product line-up) and "The Real Fourth Sector" (glossy pie).

### TS03 Split
- **Look:** two photos meeting edge to edge (no divider line, Sam 3 Nov), a label and a big number on each side, a question box centred on the seam.
- **Seen in:** Sam's reference "Third Places, Explained"; It's Complicated, "Why everything feels more expensive now" (Then/Now chocolate bars, 5.4M); Fun History, "423 Years of American History" (1492/Today, 11.9M).

### TS04 Big number + 3D object
- **Look:** a huge stakes number with a second word in the accent colour, beside a 3D render (three.js: gold, glass, paper, clay; environment light; soft shadow).
- **Seen in:** Sam's references "$2 Trillion Gone" and "The Real Fourth Sector"; Vox, "Why can't prices just stay the same?" (coin stacks, 2.5M).

### TS05 Giant word on an illustrated pattern
- **Look:** one huge condensed word filling the width, a kicker chip above and a slanted tag below, over a tone-on-tone pattern of small line icons in the palette.
- **Seen in:** LITTLE BIT BETTER, "Once You Understand Economics..." (4.0M) and "The Map of Money" (3.3M); 20 Minute University (3.2M); Wacky Science, "ALL OF PHYSICS" (11.8M).

### TS06 Icon grid (taxonomy)
- **Look:** white ground, a heavy title on top, 8-12 round labelled icons in a grid (our own Folk faces or icon set).
- **Seen in:** EverythingProfessor, "Explaining Basic Financial Concepts" (2.6M) and "Every Mental Disorder" (6.0M); The Analyst, "Every Government Form" (5.1M).

### TS07 Character reaction
- **Look:** our Folk character large, with a strong expression, reacting to the hero object or a chart; saturated flat colours; one big word.
- **Seen in:** Kurzgesagt, "Immune System" (26M), "Dumb" (5.3M), "Stop it, brain!" (8.7M); Crayon Capital, "2008 Financial Crisis" (2.4M); Casual Economics, "The Great Depression" (3.1M).

### TS08 One dramatic chart + marker note
- **Look:** graph paper, one or two thick lines with a turn or a crossing, the key point circled, a condensed headline and a hand-written note.
- **Seen in:** How Money Works, "This Economy Sucks... Why Is The Stock Market Booming?" (1.0M) and "tHe pOpULaTiOn iS CoLlapSiNg!" (833K); Casual Economics (map + red arrow).

### TS09 Document as evidence
- **Look:** a receipt, bill or payslip (drawn by us, never a real person's), one number circled in red, a sticker with the change, a short headline beside it.
- **Seen in:** Johnny Harris, "Why Groceries Are SO Expensive Now" (receipt + ▲200%, 2.5M); Degenerocity, "Everything is Expensive" (crossed-out price tag, 3.0M).

### TS10 Banknote portrait
- **Look:** our S8 note: engraved frame, a Folk character in the guilloché portrait oval, the denomination as the number (crossed out), a rubber stamp with the real figure.
- **Seen in (technique only):** Johnny Harris, "How Money Actually Works" (person on a dollar bill, 3.7M); Aperture, "Money: Humanity's Biggest Illusion" (banknote macro, 2.9M). Our own design; no real currency reproduced.

### TS11 Repetition with one odd one out
- **Look:** a wall of the same object (price tags, coins, cards) with one different item in the middle.
- **Seen in:** Business Insider, "Why Owning Nothing Is So Expensive" (wall of subscription cards, 3.7M); Sam's Bloomberg reference (rows of scooters).

### TS12 Map + flags + arrow
- **Look:** a light map, two or three flags or chips, one rising or falling arrow, a short verdict ("Not even close").
- **Seen in:** Money & Macro, "Economist explains why India can never grow like China" (1.7M); Fun History (1492/Today map).

### TS13 Documentary photo + condensed caps
- **Look:** a dark, graded real photo (free stock), yellow condensed caps on torn paper strips, a small series badge.
- **Seen in:** Economics Explained, "How Old Money Kills Economies" (740K), "Living Paycheck to Paycheck?" (860K), "Economics Is A Junk Science" (1.2M).

### TS14 Crude flat cartoon on white
- **Look:** a deliberately simple drawing of one character and one silly object on pure white, no text or one word.
- **Seen in:** Primate Economics, "Inflation Explained with Bananas" (1.8M) and "Tariffs Explained with Bananas" (1.5M).

### TS15 Big word behind the subject
- **Look:** one huge word across the frame, the character standing in front of it and covering part of it, a small tag with the twist.
- **Seen in:** Escaping Ordinary, "The Psychology of Money in 20 minutes" (MONEY behind the figure, 6.3M); Think School, "The Rise of BYD" (silhouette poster, 2.2M).

### TS16 Loud collage (not for us)
- Brand logos, game and cartoon characters and celebrity faces piled up (Diamondbolt, Bladed Angel, The Financial Diet). High energy, but it breaks our no-logos and no-faces rules.

### TS17 Annotated chart + meme panel
- **Look:** a clean chart of a real statistic on one side, a two-panel meme comparison (drawn with our Folk characters) on the other.
- **Seen in:** Cole Hastings, "The Affordability Crisis" (CPI chart + "my parents in their 20s" panel, 499K).

## Patterns from the study
- Economics and money channels lean on four moves: one giant word (TS05), a document or price as evidence (TS09), a dramatic chart (TS08) and then/now or country splits (TS03, TS12).
- Science and psychology channels lean on a big expressive character (TS07) and icon grids (TS06).
- Faceless channels with millions of views (Kurzgesagt, EverythingProfessor, Wacky Science, LITTLE BIT BETTER, PsychToons) show a face is not required: a character or one strong word does the job.
- Text is short: most use 1-3 words; titles carry the rest.
