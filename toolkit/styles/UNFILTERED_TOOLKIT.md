# Unfiltered toolkit: making the script sound raw (U01-U26)

Sam, 11 Nov 2026: "The script needs to feel like it's unfiltered and raw." Studied the same day. This sits next to `RIDICULOUS_TOOLKIT.md` (gag shapes), `PICTURE_GAGS_CATALOGUE.md` (picture gags), `SCHULZ_COMEDY_TOOLKIT.md` (S01-S24) and `BRITISH_HUMOUR_TOOLKIT.md` (B01-B30). Those teach joke *shapes*; this one is about *voice*: a real person who is a bit fed up, thinking out loud, and still right.

**The one rule over all of them:** raw is a delivery of true things, never a licence for false ones. Every successful rant voice studied builds the facts first and the anger second. John Oliver calls his show's process "the slowest improv you've ever seen". Jokes come late, after researchers check everything, because he won't "build an argument on sand" (NPR Fresh Air). Everything below goes through the fact-check like any other line, and the hedges stay ("as far as anyone can show", "potential biosignature").

## The moves

### A. The person talking
- **U01 The confession opener.** Start a section by admitting something unflattering about yourself before you attack anything. Climate Town opens on "This is gonna make me sound like I'm a thousand years old". Sam's true version is already in the script (the chartered accountant); use one more, true, per video. Never invent biography.
- **U02 The absurd credential.** Introduce yourself with one real qualification and one useless one. Climate Town's host introduces himself with his real degree and his phone's 11% battery; a top comment quoted it back ("100% battery"), so viewers remember this. Sam: "a chartered accountant with seven years of spreadsheets and zero alien contacts".
- **U03 The attitude word.** Before writing a bit, name its feeling in one word: weird, stupid, hard, scary, unfair (Carter). If a line has no attitude it reads as narration; with one it reads as a person. Put the word in the script's `emotion` card.
- **U04 Warm, not bitter.** Zakir Khan's lesson, and Sam's register: you are the friend over chai who can't believe this either, not a man shouting at a cloud. Anger goes at systems and money, the warmth at the people (Drake freezing, Ehman's red pen).
- **U05 Self-deprecation as permission.** Brooker can be savage because he is hardest on himself ("self-indulgent cry-baby"). Have Sam lose one argument to himself per chapter: "I wanted to be outraged about this. Then I checked. It's worse."

### B. Thinking out loud
- **U06 The live self-correction.** Say something, then visibly correct it: "Actually, you know what?" Climate Town: the 1900s Chicago smell wasn't the utility's fault, then the correction that it might have smelled anyway. In ours: "Nobody's coming to pick it up. Well, someone was. Then the budget happened."
- **U07 The fake double-check.** Pretend to look something up mid-sentence, then land the punch: "at the expense of, let me just double check this, you and everyone you know". Ours: "the people funding the search are, let me check, mostly... donors. And a billionaire. And us, I guess, if you count hope."
- **U08 The "also" pile-on.** One absurd fact, then "Also," another, then "Also," a third, each shorter. Climate Town: the library caught fire; also stray cats. Use it only with true items.
- **U09 The aside to a named nobody.** Break off to tell a made-up acquaintance he's wrong ("No, it's not more important than ever to come to your improv show, Kevin"). Make the name obviously fictional and never a real person.
- **U10 Interrupting yourself.** Start a sentence, abandon it, restart with the blunt version: "The mission was... look, they cancelled it." In the doc, mark it with an ellipsis so ElevenLabs pauses.

### C. Rhythm of a rant
- **U11 The slow burn** (Lewis Black). Calm statement, then the absurd fact, then the key phrase repeated twice, tighter each time, then a short pause, then the blow-up line. Once or twice a video, never in a row. In ours: "Seventy-two minutes. Seventy-two. *Minutes.*" (`[shouting]` only on the last one).
- **U12 The one-word verdict.** After a chart or a big comparison, one dry word: "Different." "Cool." "Awesome." Climate Town does it after a two-bar chart. Our bar chart in the opening is built for it, in a later chapter's line.
- **U13 Sarcastic restatement.** Restate the mechanism in its plainest, most damning form: "you are paying extra so that your utility can make you pay even more extra. Awesome." Ours: "So the robots eat the budget, and then the robots help with the search. Thanks, robots."
- **U14 Escalating literal analogy.** A plain analogy, then one notch further: the Yankees against a high-school team, then "or the Pittsburgh Pirates or something". Ours: "a lunch-break budget... a short lunch... a snack".
- **U15 Write for the ear** (Carlin). Read every line aloud. Hard consonants on the punch word, the punch word last (Carter: the key word at the end of the sentence), and the rhythm of three with the third one breaking.
- **U16 Kill the corporate phrase.** Carlin kept a file of "the way we talk" and attacked buzzwords. Quote the official phrase, then translate it: "a 'strategic reprioritisation'. Meaning: no."

### D. Structure
- **U17 Facts first, jokes last.** Write each chapter joke-free until its argument holds, then add the jokes. A joke written on a figure that later changes dies with it (Oliver).
- **U18 A thesis per chapter** (Minhaj). Say the take in one blunt line near the top: "This chapter is about how NASA found a maybe-alien rock and then couldn't afford the postage."
- **U19 Serious beats on purpose.** Plan where the jokes stop. Raw doesn't mean every line is a joke; the dead-straight line after a run of jokes is what makes the next one land, and what makes it feel honest. About one straight beat per 30-45 s.
- **U20 Quantity, then cut** (Onion). Write 5-10 versions of each key joke line, keep one. Dikkers puts it bluntly: "95% of everything we create is garbage." Do this offline; Sam sees only the winner.
- **U21 The sober voice for the absurd claim** (Onion). For fake headlines, press releases and memos, write in a flat newspaper tone; never wink. "Area Civilisation Spends 72 Minutes On Biggest Question In History."

### E. Raw texture (delivery)
- **U22 Swears for rhythm, not shock.** In Sam's register (research-story section 6): a swear lands on the last beat of a rant or replaces an adjective, never in a claim or a quote. Climate Town keeps the strong ones rare and the mild ones casual ("sucks super bad").
- **U23 Fillers, sparingly.** One "look," "honestly," "okay so" opens a turn. More than about one a minute reads as padding; Sam's cuts of 11 Nov removed exactly those.
- **U24 Breaths and sighs.** ElevenLabs tags like `[sighs]` or `[exhales]` before a resigned line make it sound unscripted. Test each in a 10-s ElevenLabs sample first: Sam's banned list stands (`[curious]`, `[softly]` and the rest), and at stability 100% tags only half work.
- **U25 The blooper tail.** Climate Town ends on outtakes after the credits. For us: 10-20 s of Sam's real flubbed takes or a "director's note" card after the "Next time" card. Only his real audio, only if he wants it.
- **U26 The audience talks back.** Answer the viewer's imagined objection out loud: "And before you type it: yes, company money and government money are different pots. I checked. Still weird."

## What raw is not
- Not inventing a fact, a quote, a number or a person to make it angrier.
- Not punching down: no jokes on identity; targets are institutions, money, ourselves.
- Not one long shout: Lewis Black is calm most of the time, which is why the eruption works.
- Not cruelty at private people. Brooker's own warning: once you feel for the target, the vicious line fails.
- Not swearing in packaging (titles, thumbnails, descriptions).

## Checklist for an "unfiltered pass" on a chapter
1. One attitude word per bit, in the emotion card (U03).
2. At least one thinking-out-loud move (U06, U07 or U10) and one rant rhythm (U11-U14).
3. One straight, serious beat after the biggest joke run (U19).
4. Read every changed line aloud for rhythm (U15); punch word last.
5. Count: fillers about 1 a minute at most (U23), swears within Sam's budget (U22).
6. Fact-check every changed line; hedges untouched.
7. Deliver as doc suggestions; never touch a chapter Sam has locked.
