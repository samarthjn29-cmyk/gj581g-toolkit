# Picture gags catalogue (GJ 581g)

Deep study for Sam, 11 Nov 2026. It collects every picture gag we could find and how to build each one in the stills format. Companion to `styles/RIDICULOUS_TOOLKIT.md`.

**Sources studied:**
- **Every Frame a Painting**, "Edgar Wright – How to Do Visual Comedy" (full transcript, 11M views).
- **David Bordwell**, "Funny framings".
- **TV Tropes:** about 150 comedy and visual trope pages, including the whole comedy-tropes index filtered for visual devices.
- **explain xkcd:** the SETI, Aliens, Charts, Scale, Mars-rover and red-annotation categories.
- **Saturday Morning Breakfast Cereal (SMBC):** interviews with Zach Weinersmith.
- **Bob Mankoff** on New Yorker cartoons.
- **Scott McCloud**, *Understanding Comics*.
- **John Vorhaus**, *The Comic Toolbox*.
- **Chuck Jones's Road Runner rules.**
- **Know Your Meme:** formats, used for structure only.

We take the **shape** of each gag, never anyone's drawing, clip, character or catchphrase.

**Build key:**
- **K:** our kit can do it today (camera moves, cards, stamp, rain, flash, pip, aside, banner, starburst, headline, triple zoom, shake, sounds).
- **N:** needs a small new piece (most take under an hour).
- **AI:** needs one new AI still.
- **S:** stock or public-domain picture.

## A. Framing and camera (Edgar Wright and Bordwell)
The video's thesis: most comedy today is "lightly edited improv", with jokes carried only by dialogue. The frame is a playground, so play.

| # | Gag | How it works | Build |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Something pops into frame** | An object or character enters suddenly from an edge, ideally on a sound | K (pip / stamp from the edge) · N (slide-in cut-out) |
| A2 | **Someone leaves the frame funnily** | Exit too fast, wrong way, or through the floor | N (cut-out exit) |
| A3 | **There and back again** | A character leaves and comes straight back, usually having changed their mind or with something absurd | N |
| A4 | **Matching transitions** | Cut on a matching shape: a round dish becomes a pizza, becomes a stopwatch | K (cut on shape; choose pictures with matching shapes) |
| A5 | **Perfectly timed sound effect** | The sound lands on the frame of the action and does half the joke | K |
| A6 | **Action synced to the music** | Cuts or zooms land exactly on beats | K (cue zooms to the bed's beats) |
| A7 | **Super-dramatic lighting cue** | Lights snap off and a spotlight falls on someone saying something trivial | N (darken + spotlight vignette overlay) |
| A8 | **Fence gag** | The camera pans behind a pillar or fence; when it comes out the other side, something has changed | N (pan + wipe-swap behind a foreground object) |
| A9 | **Imaginary action** | A character mimes a fight and the sound effects play as if it's real | Sound only, K |
| A10 | **The laugh zoom** | A slow or snap zoom onto a reaction, or onto a detail nobody noticed | K |

## J. Comic-strip craft (McCloud, Mankoff, SMBC, Vorhaus, Road Runner)
- **Beat panel:** a silent picture just before the punchline. For us it's a still held 1 s with no words and the music dipped.
- **Closure in the gutter (McCloud):** show the "before" and the "after" and let the viewer imagine the middle. The **offscreen crash** is the classic: the sound plays over a calm shot, then cut to the wreckage. Funnier than showing the crash.
- **McCloud's six transitions:** moment-to-moment, action-to-action, subject-to-subject, scene-to-scene, aspect-to-aspect and non-sequitur. Comedy lives in the last two: a cut that seems unrelated until the brain connects it.
- **Word-picture "parallel" (McCloud):** words and picture tell two different stories. That's the unreliable voiceover (B15) and our strongest satire device: the narration is solemn, the picture is ridiculous.
- **Incongruity + cognitive mash-up (Mankoff):** one picture where two worlds collide (a boardroom of penguins, aliens at a pension office), with the caption as the last piece that makes it click.
- **Let the premise be the joke (Weinersmith):** real knowledge plus an absurd but logical payoff. SMBC also hides a bonus joke after the end (the "votey").
- **Clash of context (Vorhaus):** take something out of its world and put it where it doesn't fit. Also: comic premise = the gap between comic reality and real reality; exaggeration; rule of three; inappropriate response; tension and release.
- **Chuck Jones's Road Runner rules** (as usually quoted; one collaborator said he never saw them written down):
  - The Road Runner never harms the Coyote directly.
  - The Coyote's own inventions and plans defeat him.
  - All his gadgets come from one company, ACME.
  - Gravity is his greatest enemy.
  - He's humiliated more than harmed.
  - The audience's sympathy stays with him.
  - No dialogue beyond "beep-beep".
  - The action stays in one natural setting.

  For us, the "Coyote" is humanity's alien search. It's always defeated by its own budget, its own mistakes and its own one supplier, never by the universe. Keep the sympathy with the searchers.
- **xkcd 638 "The Search":** ants search two kitchen tiles for pheromones, find nothing, and conclude they're alone. A perfect one-picture analogy for how little of the sky we've checked.
- **xkcd 2572 "Alien Observers":** the aliens keep moving their "no-fly zone" every time humans get a better camera. A perfect gag for UFO footage that's always blurry.

## Where these go in the Aliens script (shortlist, about 30)
**Opening** (picture only; the script is locked):
- B14 B-roll rebus on "shiny things": shiny objects flash by, one per word.
- E6 the bar chart: "100 million" vs "730 billion". The bar runs off the top of the screen and the camera tilts up and up.

**Ch 1, the cold box:**
- A21 "down here" shot: the huge Green Bank dish, then tilt down to a tiny figure in the box.
- G16 unusually uninteresting sight: a plane flying over the telescope, labelled "the signal".
- I6 a dun-dun-DUN when the "signal" is revealed to be the plane.
- H2 brick joke: the same plane flies across the very last shot of the video.

**Ch 2, Wow!:**
- D18 red annotations on the real Wow! printout, our own: "6EQUJ5 = WOW", "this cost $0".
- C2 delayed reaction: Ehman's red pen circle, then a beat, then "Wow!" stamped huge.
- E9 evidence matrix: the Wow! signal on the "strength of evidence" axis, with reactions from "IT'S ALIENS" to "probably hydrogen".

**Ch 3, hunting season:**
- B2 Gilligan cut: "It was meant to run for a decade." Cut: an OFF switch being flipped.
- D13 headline: "Congress Bravely Defeats Aliens".
- F7 comically small demand: the whole search's first-year cost shown as one tiny coin next to a giant Senate building.
- E22 / G2 the due-diligence scorecard as an embarrassing slide: the last slide is the popcorn alien holding a "PLEASE" sign.

**Ch 4, leopard spots:**
- G3 boring vacation slideshow: "Perseverance's holiday photos": rock, rock, rock, rock with spots (gasp), rock.
- G7 ironic juxtaposition: an "INVESTING IN DISCOVERY" billboard over the abandoned sample tube.
- E21 progress bar: "Mars sample return: 0%… CANCELLED".
- K expectation vs reality: little green men vs a stain.

**Ch 5, follow the money:**
- E7 the xkcd-style scale chart: NASA science as a stack of squares, astrobiology as one square, big tech's spending as a block so big the camera pulls back and back.
- D14 proportional article importance: "DATA CENTRE SALE!" huge, "Mars rock may hold signs of life" in tiny print.
- K two buttons: "fund the search" or "one more data centre".
- B15 unreliable voiceover on "Company money and government money come out of different pots": two pots, and one is clearly bigger. It stays factual, so it's fine.
- I3 left the background music on: the jaunty music plays under the AI-priority line until the narrator says "can we turn that off?"

**Ch 6, the new listener:**
- H4 overly long gag: eight robot babies pop up one by one, then all fall over.
- E10 Up Goer Five: the AI explained in simple words, "thinking box that reads old sky noise".
- K expanding-brain ladder: listen → look for stains → ask a robot → ask the robot if it's lonely.

**Ch 7, the end:**
- H1 the "still no aliens" tally hits 4; the counter falls off the screen.
- D13 headline "Update: It's Just Us".
- H6 stinger after the dare: three seconds of night sky, then a faint "beep-beep" of an incoming signal and a hard cut to black. It stays honest, because it's clearly a joke, with no claim.
- No gags from Hawking to the dare: the ending stays sincere.

**Running all video:**
- G13 spot-the-alien: the popcorn alien hidden in one picture per chapter.
- E20 the tally card.

### New pieces this would need, ranked by usefulness for the series
1. **Chart kit:** animated bar chart with tilt-up, scale squares, line graph, pie, Venn, flowchart, matrix. **The biggest win:** it's core to a money-and-science channel and used in every video. About 3–4 hours.
2. **Cut-out mover:** a cut-out sliding, popping, exiting, a clown-car stream, a talking flap head. About 2 hours (cut-outs via Recraft's background removal).
3. **Sign and card set:** road sign, telegram, clock face, slideshow frame, progress bar, tally counter, red-marker annotation layer, censor bar. About 2–3 hours.
4. **Split-screen reaction grid** and **spotlight lighting cue.** About 1 hour.
5. **Meme-structure cards:** expectation/reality, tier list, iceberg, alignment chart, ladder, two buttons. About 2 hours.
