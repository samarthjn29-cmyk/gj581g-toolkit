# South Park study: storytelling and visuals (11 Nov 2026)

Sam asked for a study of South Park's storytelling and visual elements. Three official clips from the South Park Studios YouTube channel were sampled at 60 frames each, across the whole clip, and their transcripts were read. Matt Stone and Trey Parker's own clip on their writing process was also read, along with their "but / therefore" rule. **We take shapes only:** never their characters, look, designs, songs or catchphrases. Their signature move of pasting real people's photo faces onto cartoon bodies is off limits for us, because we never doctor a real person's likeness.

## Storytelling
1. **But / therefore, never "and then."** Parker and Stone's stated rule: every beat is joined to the next by "but" or "therefore". If beats are joined by "and then", the story is just a list. Our chapter 1 already runs this way: he's cold (but) the budget is nothing (therefore) cheap is the plan (but) a signal (but) it's a plane. **Use:** check every chapter's beat list for this rule before scripting.
2. **The take is found, not planned.** "We won't even know how we feel about it until Tuesday." They pick a big topic and rewrite until the angle reveals itself; the joke arrives late ("that's the joke"). For us: research first, and let the satire come out of the facts. Never start from a message and hunt for facts to fit it.
3. **The escalating running gag ("6-7").**
   - The same small gag is used four times by four characters, each set-up more contrived (a time, a homework problem, a sneak attack, a medical test).
   - Then an authority figure bans it.
   - Then the authority figure says it himself, by accident, at the climax ("Revelation 6, 67").
   - Shape: **repeat it three or more times, each time more forced; have someone ban it; then the person who banned it says it at the climax.**
4. **Same graphic, reversed meaning (the "fake news alert").** The exact same news sting that announced the scandal comes back, now saying "FAKE NEWS". The repeat is the joke. For us: our own masthead can issue a **CORRECTION** with the same banner ("A SIGNAL!" → "CORRECTION: A PLANE").
5. **Genre switch as the punchline (the PSA).** A flat cartoon scene cuts hard to the grammar of a glossy advert: lens flare, slow motion, a gold serif title, a "01 OF 50" counter card, and a tagline on black at the end ("He gets us."). Sincere form, absurd content.
6. **The chanted introduction.** The guest is introduced by a repeated sing-song line, three times, before he speaks. That's a rule of three used as an entrance.
7. **Reaction cutaways as punctuation.** After almost every claim there is a silent wide shot of the audience staring. It's the "beat" that lets a line land, used every 5–10 s.

## Visuals
- **Flat and front-on.** The camera barely moves: no pans or zooms, symmetrical staging, and characters face the viewer. The energy is all in the cuts, roughly every 2–3 s, alternating a wide audience shot, a medium shot of the speaker and a close-up.
- **The slide-talk triptych ("6-7").** A speaker on a stage with **three framed screens** behind him. The screens change every 2–3 s, mixing real photos, old public-domain paintings (Renaissance hell scenes), crude diagrams, and one big red block-letter "slide". This is essentially our own stills format, staged as a lecture.
- **Crude scale diagrams.** A ruler and a paperclip on a flat yellow ground to show something is tiny. The literal, schoolroom diagram is the joke.
- **TV-news grammar, complete:**
  - a spinning 3D title band ("SPECIAL ALERT");
  - lower-third banners that change with every beat;
  - a live inset with REC and a ticking timecode;
  - a call-in box: one static photo plus a phone icon, held while the caller talks;
  - the same set later shown small, on a TV in a living room, to cut from studio to home.
- **Black counter card.** Plain computer-style type on black ("01 OF 50") gives a series feel and a deadpan beat.
- **The style clash.** Cartoon flatness meets photoreal footage or photos. The contrast between the two looks is a gag in itself.

## What we can use (new for our kit)
1. **The "NOTHING" counter (from the 01 OF 50 card plus the running gag).** Every time "And the universe said… nothing" comes back, a small black counter card follows the crickets: "NOTHING · 1", "NOTHING · 2"… It lands at the chapter 1, 2 and 6 callbacks; chapter 6 adds "Again." Build: a `counter`-style text card on black, about 1.5 s, no new art.
2. **The correction banner (from the fake news alert).** The same masthead or burst returns with "CORRECTION:". Chapter 1: "A SIGNAL!" → "CORRECTION: A PLANE." Build: reuse `starburst`/`banner` with a red CORRECTION strap and a record-scratch sound.
3. **The slide-talk triptych.** Three framed screens on a lecture stage, with our stills swapping in on cue words. It fits chapter 3's "let's do the due diligence on aliens, like it's an investment" (cost / time / chance / upside) and chapter 5's money lines. Build: a new card `triptych` (a stage background plus three frames, images swapped on cues), with a projector click on each swap.
4. **The mock-advert switch (from the PSA).** A hard cut to a glossy photoreal still with lens flare, a gold serif title and a tagline on black. Chapter 5: "very large warm boxes that think" as a fake data-centre advert: a glowing server hall, then on black "Very large warm boxes. That think." Build from existing parts: `glint`, `title`, and a black end card. No real people or brands.
5. **The crude scale diagram.** A ruler on a flat yellow ground. Chapter 5: NASA's whole year of science against "four days" of tech spending, as a ruler with a tiny tick. Build: an SVG card `ruler`.
6. **The call-in box.** One static photo of our popcorn alien in a phone-in box with a phone icon and an "ON THE LINE" lower third, for the alien's lines ("human have money…"). Build: `pip` plus a lower third.
7. **Escalate one running gag across the video**, 6-7 style: "And the universe said… nothing" goes plain → shouted → "Again." → and at the end, the dare ("give it seventy-two seconds") hands the line to the viewer.

## Rules
- Shapes only: never draw a South Park character, mascot, font or set, and never use their songs or catchphrases.
- No doctored faces of real people (their photo-head collage technique is out). Real people appear only in unedited real photos.
- No sexual or degrading content about anyone. No news-network logos; our parody news uses our own masthead, "THE DAILY SHRUG".
- A running gag sits on a true fact every time it returns.
