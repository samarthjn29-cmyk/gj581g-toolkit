# Ridiculous toolkit (GJ 581g)

Study for Sam, 11 Nov 2026, after the "most ridiculous version" of the Aliens opening. The goal is to be as silly as possible while every fact stays true and the main point stays clear. We borrow **techniques** only, never other people's clips, characters, jingles or catchphrases.

## 1. The six sources and what each teaches

| Source | What it does | What we take |
|---|---|---|

## 2. Rules that keep it ridiculous *and* right
1. **Facts stay true.** The gag goes on top of the fact, never instead of it. A stamp, headline or starburst can't state a number or claim the video doesn't.
2. **Punch up.** Mock budgets, institutions, big tech, the universe, ourselves. Never the scientists, and never a real person's looks.
3. **One swapped word.** Write the sober version first, then swap one word (the Onion rule).
4. **Contrast is the engine.** Cheerful music or sting under a bleak fact; a calm picture under a swear; a deadpan line after chaos. The silence after the gag is part of the gag.
5. **Escalate.** Each gag a bit bigger than the last, then one quiet beat, then the biggest at the end of the section.
6. **Callbacks.** A gag that comes back pays twice (the short film, "shiny things" → "shiny little babies").
7. **Dose.** The clean video gets at most about one sight gag every 8–10 s and 8 sound effects a minute. The ridiculous cut can go twice that, but keep the voice on top and every word audible.
8. **Ours only.** Our own pictures (stock, public domain, AI where allowed), in-house synthesised sounds or CC0 recordings, our own catchphrases. No meme clips, no TV footage, no brand logos.

## 3. What the toolkit can already do (11 Nov 2026)
- **Camera moves:** push, pullback, pan, tilt, drift, punch (snap zoom), whip (blurred pan in), spin, crash (zoom out from very close), bounce, dutch (tilted); plus a decaying shake on any shot.
- **Overlays:** stamp (rubber stamp slam), rain (falling money), flash (white or colour frame), pip (reaction picture popping into a corner); yellow/green number cards, static or word-by-word.
- **Sounds (in-house):** boo (synth and a real CC0 crowd), sad trombone, ka-ching, party poppers, cheer (CC0), kiss, air horn, record scratch, drumroll with crash, boom, slide whistle, dun-dun-DUNNN.

## 4. To build next (in order of laughs per hour of work)
1. **Triple zoom** (move `triple`): 3 snaps + stings + a flash on the third. About 1 hour.
2. **Tiny text pop** (overlay `aside`): lowercase serif, under 1 s, anywhere in frame. About 30 min.
3. **Jingle sting**: 4–6 synthesised 1–2 s happy synth bursts (major arpeggio, "ta-daa", kazoo-ish). About 1 hour.
4. **Headline card**: a fake-newspaper front page in sober type, our own masthead (no real paper's name or look). About 1 hour.
5. **Breaking banner + ticker** (our own colours, no channel branding). About 1 hour.
6. **Starburst** and **scorecard**. About 1 hour each.
7. **Whoosh, thunder sting, boing** sounds for whips, triple zooms and bounces. About 30 min.

## 6. More ideas from comedy-editing craft (11 Nov 2026)
Reddit can't be searched from here (the site doesn't allow it), so these come from editors' guides on the open web.
- **Instant replay.** Freeze, rewind, and replay the punchline in slow motion with a sports-commentator sting. For us: the Ozma "signal" turning out to be a plane, replayed at half speed with a circle drawn round the plane.
- **The sudden stop.** Cut the music dead one beat before the punchline, and let the line land in silence. We already do it before "Yeah, who doesn't love data centres"; use it before "Congress switched it off" and "nobody's coming to pick it up".
- **It was there all along.** A reveal that recasts an earlier shot. The popcorn alien could already be sitting in the "empty" cinema, tiny in the back row, before the cut to it.
- **Focus on the minor detail.** Zoom past the subject to something small: the three coins in the busker's cup, the "Price: one hundred million dollars" line on the Daily Shrug.
- **Arrive late, leave early.** Start each gag mid-action and cut out the moment it lands; trim the gap between setup and punchline to the frame.
- **First line clearly funny, biggest laugh last.** Each chapter opens with a small laugh so viewers know they're allowed to laugh, and its biggest gag sits at the end of the chapter, never the middle.
- **Stop before it runs out.** If the next beat doesn't escalate, cut the bit. The ridiculous cut is a menu to pick from, not a target.
- **Don't let absurd and sincere bleed.** Keep a clear boundary: the chaos is on top of the facts, and the sincere moments (Hawking, the dare) get no gags at all.
- **Sound carries a gag without a picture.** When there's no good image, an off-screen sound does the job (a door creak on "a knock at the door", a phone ring on "voicemail").
- **Test it on a fresh viewer.** Show a cut to someone who hasn't seen it and watch where they laugh or drift. We can use a fresh reviewer agent the same way for pacing.

### The 15 gag shapes, and where each goes in the Aliens script
1. **Casual understatement of a catastrophe.** "Oops, Arecibo fell down." In ch3 (now cut) or ch4: "And the mission to bring it home was cancelled. Oops."
2. **One line SHOUTED.** In ch2: "and then it was gone. GONE." (big caps card + crash zoom). One per video.
3. **The narrator interrupts himself.** In ch5: "You could build a whole religion around data centr- no. Don't."
4. **Institutions with moods.** Congress, NASA and big tech drawn as moody characters: "Congress, who did not feel like it".
5. **Running gag with tiny variations.** "Still no aliens" counter: a little tally card in the corner each time a search comes up empty (plane, Wow!, eight signals…), ticking up.
6. **Huge outcome, tiny cause.** "A whole national search, switched off by one good joke."
7. **Recurring formula sentence.** Our own: "And the universe said… nothing." after each failed search (ch1, ch2, ch6).
8. **Hidden-object game.** The popcorn alien hidden somewhere in a picture in every chapter; one line in the description: "spot the alien".
9. **Disproportionate reaction to the mundane.** A stadium crowd roaring at "High Resolution Microwave Survey"; a choir on "spreadsheet".
10. **Escalation with a fixed deadpan answer.** Ch4 or ch7: "A rock with leopard spots. Minerals linked to microbes. NASA's chief calls it the closest yet. Have we found life? …Can't really tell." It's true to the evidence and funny, and the strongest fit we found.
11. **Absurd innocent explanation.** Ch2: "Maybe the Wow! signal was just a very loud alien sneeze." Labelled as a joke, straight after the real explanations.
12. **One mascot, dumb-simple summary.** The popcorn alien sums up each chapter in caveman-simple words ("Human have money. Human buy computer. Alien sad.").
13. **Breezy wave-away.** In ch4 for the sample-return cancellation: "Bring the sample home? Eh. Too expensive. Next."
14. **Blunt literal description.** Data centres as "very large warm boxes that think"; the Drake equation as "a list of shrugs".
15. **Genre parody of ourselves.** The ch7 outro starts in full sincere-explainer voice, swerves into absurdity for one line, then lands sincere for real at Hawking.

Rules from the study: the most-liked moments are almost all **writing, not effects**. The edit's job is timing: a pause before, a hard cut after, and silence where the joke needs room. Sound and zooms only sell a line that's already funny.
