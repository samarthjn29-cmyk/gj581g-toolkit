# Ridiculous toolkit (GJ 581g)

Study for Sam, 11 Nov 2026, after the "most ridiculous version" of the Aliens opening. The goal is to be as silly as possible while every fact stays true and the main point stays clear. We borrow **techniques** only, never other people's clips, characters, jingles or catchphrases.

## 1. The six sources and what each teaches

| Source | What it does | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Bill Wurtz** ("history of the entire world, i guess") | Real history told at speed, with all-lowercase serif text flashing on screen, crude clip-art next to low-poly 3D and stock photos, synth noodling under the voice that bursts into 1–2 word jingles, cheerful music under bleak facts, and tiny blink-and-miss text gags | The **tiny text pop** (a 2–6 word aside in lowercase, on screen for under 1 s); the **jingle sting** (a 1–2 s happy synth burst on a bleak fact); **mixed media** (a real photo with a cartoon arrow and clip-art on top); the **overlong gag** (one joke pushed past its natural length, once per video) |
| **Indian daily-soap drama edits** | The same shocked face hit with zoom after zoom, a thunder/"dhan" sting on each, colour flashes, re-cuts of the reaction, the shot held long | The **triple zoom**: 3 snap zooms at 0.0 / 0.35 / 0.7 s on one reaction picture, each with its own sting, a red/white flash on the third, then a slow push while it sinks in. It answers Sam's "variety in zooms", since it's a rhythm, not a single move |
| **Hindi primetime news graphics** | Permanent urgency: a red BREAKING band, a scrolling ticker, a spinning 3D headline, whooshes and flames on every number, a dramatic music bed | The **breaking banner** + **ticker** for one mock-serious moment (e.g. "BREAKING: ALIEN SEARCH BUDGET FOUND DOWN BACK OF SOFA"); the **number slam** (a figure flies in with fire and a whoosh) |
| **The Onion** | Headlines are written first, in a sober newspaper voice, and only the best survive. Research on Onion headlines found the joke usually sits in **one swapped word** that is like the real word but different in a key way ("spilling" for "drilling"), with oppositions such as sublime vs mundane or modern vs outdated | **Headline cards** in sober newspaper type with one swapped word: "Area Civilisation Spends 72 Minutes On Biggest Question In History". Write 10, keep 1 |
| **Late-night infomercials / 90s local TV ads** | Starbursts, "BUT WAIT, THERE'S MORE", fake testimonials, terrible WordArt | The **starburst** on a deal ("ONLY $100M! 10 YEARS!"). We never fake testimonials from real or invented people presented as real |
| **Taskmaster-style mock solemnity** | Serious scoring of silly things, cheap props, a straight face | The **scorecard**: Sam's chartered-accountant due diligence as an on-screen audit sheet, each line stamped (COST: TINY ✔ · ODDS: ??? · UPSIDE: EVERYTHING) |

## 2. Rules that keep it ridiculous *and* right
1. **Facts stay true.** The gag goes on top of the fact, never instead of it. A stamp, headline or starburst can't state a number or claim the video doesn't.
2. **Punch up.** Mock budgets, institutions, big tech, the universe, ourselves. Never the scientists, and never a real person's looks.
3. **One swapped word.** Write the sober version first, then swap one word (the Onion rule).
4. **Contrast is the engine.** Cheerful music or sting under a bleak fact; a calm picture under a swear; a deadpan line after chaos. The silence after the gag is part of the gag.
5. **Escalate.** Each gag a bit bigger than the last, then one quiet beat, then the biggest at the end of the section.
6. **Callbacks.** A gag that comes back pays twice (the short film, "shiny things" → "shiny little babies").
7. **Dose.** The clean video gets at most about one sight gag every 8–10 s and 8 sound effects a minute. The ridiculous cut can go twice that, but keep the voice on top and every word audible.
8. **Ours only.** Our own pictures (stock, public domain, AI where allowed), in-house synthesised sounds or CC0 recordings, our own catchphrases. No meme clips, no TV footage, no brand logos.

## 3. What the toolkit can already do (11 Nov 2026)
- **Camera moves:** push, pullback, pan, tilt, drift, punch (snap zoom), whip (blurred pan in), spin, crash (zoom out from very close), bounce, dutch (tilted); plus a decaying shake on any shot.
- **Overlays:** stamp (rubber stamp slam), rain (falling money), flash (white or colour frame), pip (reaction picture popping into a corner); yellow/green number cards, static or word-by-word.
- **Sounds (in-house):** boo (synth and a real CC0 crowd), sad trombone, ka-ching, party poppers, cheer (CC0), kiss, air horn, record scratch, drumroll with crash, boom, slide whistle, dun-dun-DUNNN.

## 4. To build next (in order of laughs per hour of work)
1. **Triple zoom** (move `triple`): 3 snaps + stings + a flash on the third. About 1 hour.
2. **Tiny text pop** (overlay `aside`): lowercase serif, under 1 s, anywhere in frame. About 30 min.
3. **Jingle sting**: 4–6 synthesised 1–2 s happy synth bursts (major arpeggio, "ta-daa", kazoo-ish). About 1 hour.
4. **Headline card**: a fake-newspaper front page in sober type, our own masthead (no real paper's name or look). About 1 hour.
5. **Breaking banner + ticker** (our own colours, no channel branding). About 1 hour.
6. **Starburst** and **scorecard**. About 1 hour each.
7. **Whoosh, thunder sting, boing** sounds for whips, triple zooms and bounces. About 30 min.

## 5. Where these land in the Aliens script (ideas, not yet built)
- **Opening, "these tech companies":** breaking banner, "BREAKING: TECH COMPANIES STILL TECH COMPANIES".
- **Opening, "$100 million… for ten years":** starburst "ONLY $100M! TEN WHOLE YEARS!" + kazoo jingle.
- **Opening, "Can you effin believe that?":** triple zoom on the popcorn alien.
- **Ch 1, the plane:** tiny text pop on the plane photo, "(it was a plane)".
- **Ch 2, the Wow! signal:** jingle sting on "Wow!", then a long silence.
- **Ch 3, Congress switches it off:** headline card "Congress Bravely Defeats Aliens"; the senator's quote stays as a quote, on screen with its source.
- **Ch 3, due diligence:** the scorecard.
- **Ch 4, Mars sample left behind:** starburst "FREE PARCEL! NOBODY COLLECTING!"
- **Ch 5, four days:** number slam with fire; "long weekend" with a beach-chair clip-art pop.
- **Ch 6, robots join the search:** overlong gag. The robot babies keep finding "signals", and each one turns out to be another plane.
- **Ch 7, press release:** headline card "Update: It's Just Us". Then the music drops out entirely for the Hawking line, because the end needs to land sincere.
