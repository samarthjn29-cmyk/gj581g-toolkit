# Backgrounds and Johnny Harris-style editing: six videos (23 Nov 2026)

Sam asked for these six videos to be analysed, then: "I am starting to feel that it gets a bit boring when you use only one background for all your frames. Review the videos above and see how we can bring variety to our backgrounds by using different things."

The videos were studied in the built-in browser with the sound muted. Method: transcripts, canvas frame sheets, and change-rate samples taken every 0.5 s. No clips or frames were kept.

## What these videos do with backgrounds
None of them keeps one ground. The ground changes with the job of the shot.

1. **Paper desk** (Faceless, byl). A cream or white sheet with a grunge texture in multiply, a warm grade, film dust in screen blend, and a slow zoom-out on the texture itself. Used for documents, memos, cut-outs and title words.
2. **Kraft map ground** (Faceless). A tan, desaturated map fills the frame and becomes the stage. Cut-out planes, a dotted route, labels and a filled triangle all sit on it. The scene happens on the map, not in front of a canvas.
3. **Cartographic map ground that travels** (Johnny Harris, Coups and Sudan). A light real-cartography map is the background for most of the video. It pans and zooms from region to region, and countries fill with colour. Tokens sit on it: octagon portraits, round icons (people, a motive, an organisation), flow arcs between countries, and a year-dial scrubber at the bottom.
4. **Dark physical map under a light** (Sudan, 200 s). The same map in black and white, lit by a vignette spot. It suits a change of mood: history, or "how did we get here".
5. **Blurred copy of the same photo** (byl clip 14). The photo, enlarged and blurred behind the sharp version. The blur eases to 0% on a camera push.
6. **Collage pile** (Faceless, 647-652 s). Clippings, photos and pages scattered, blurred and dimmed as the ground for a sharp card or tag.
7. **Frame within a frame** (Faceless, 603 s; Johnny Harris footage at 700 s). A rounded card inset on a deep ground (navy with specks), or footage in a rounded film frame on black. The ground is the space around the card.
8. **Film strip / contact sheet** (byl and Mapal). Photos in sprocketed frames on black, with frame numbers. The camera slides along it, with a red circle and an arrow drawn on.
9. **Toned grungy metal** (Mapal). A metal texture in hard-light blend over a tinted solid. Every photo is tritoned to the ground's colours so they belong together.
10. **The host's real room** (Coups, 150 s). A wall map, shelves, a person. We have no presenter, so this one is out.

## Other techniques worth keeping (not backgrounds)
- **Counting people as icons:** "13 MEN" with a row of 13 small figures, then a red X struck over the plane cut-out with "NEVER COMES BACK" (Faceless).
- **Luggage-tag label:** a tag reading "NOTHING EXTRA" on a cut-out (Faceless). Our parcel tag in VIS-049 is the same idea.
- **Memo spotlight** (byl clip 18): the memo slides up with motion blur; every word but the key ones fades; the key words are retyped on top; a yellow highlighter wipes across them in multiply.
- **Name card** (byl clip 6): a rounded black-and-white portrait, the name in bold, the role in regular, and vertical text along the edge.
- **Hand-drawn route on a tilted 3D map** (byl clip 7, Mapal): a rough red line that boils, then an X that marks the spot, then a push into the X.
- **Actor network** (Sudan): a hub portrait linked to icon badges for its resources (gold, weapons, money), plus coloured support arcs between countries.
- **Case-file sidebar** (Coups): TARGET / US-BACKED LEADER / MOTIVE / MAIN CHARACTERS filled per case, beside the map, with a running list of cases.
- **Music as a place marker** (Coups): the bed changes genre with each location (Hawaiian, percussive, quaint piano, urgent synth).
- **The finish, Mapal's "sauce":** animation posterised to 12 fps; a slight camera shake (wiggle, posterised to 6 fps); a slight RGB split at the edges (chroma warp); focus-hunting blur at match cuts; fractal film dust; grain in overlay. Match cuts are driven by motion: the old scene scales and moves out in the same direction the new one comes in.

## Proposal for us: a ground library, with one rule

### Grounds (each would be a register row with a best use and limit)
- **LOK-027 PaperDesk.** A cream paper sheet with procedural fibre and grunge (TEX-007 plus a new grunge layer), a warm grade and a slow texture zoom. For pages, memos, typed lines and the ObjectStory parcel.
- **MAP-011 MapGround.** Our registered period map (SRC-033 Bowen 1760), or a later map, as a full-frame stage with pan and zoom between places. Tokens and routes sit on it. For any line that names a place (Kendal, Eaglesfield, Manchester, London).
- **MAP-012 LitMapGround.** The same map, desaturated and dark under a vignette spot. For act openings and "how we got here" lines.
- **LOK-028 BlurPlate.** A blurred, darkened copy of the shot's own photo behind the sharp card, easing to sharp on the push. For portrait and photo shots, so the ground carries the photo's own colour.
- **VIS-050 CollageGround.** The episode's registered sources, scattered, blurred and dimmed, behind a sharp card or line. For "the record says", "the story tells it" and recap lines.
- **LOK-029 InsetFrame.** A rounded card on a deep chapter-tinted ground with slow dust (LOK-026's act colour at low strength). For footage and the act's held hero image.
- **VIS-051 ContactStrip.** Sprocketed film-strip frames of registered photos, a slide along the strip, and a drawn circle with an arrow. For "years of photographs" and comparisons over time.
- **Warm graphite stays** for diagrams and data (cones, spectra, dot fields), where the light and colour mixing need a dark ground.

### The rule (proposed)
- No ground holds for more than 2 shots in a row.
- An act uses at least 4 grounds, and graphite at most about 40% of its runtime.
- The ground follows the shot's job: place → map, document or object → paper, person or photo → blur plate, evidence → collage, data or light → graphite.
- A Gate 1 check flags any act that breaks this, the same way stillness is checked.
