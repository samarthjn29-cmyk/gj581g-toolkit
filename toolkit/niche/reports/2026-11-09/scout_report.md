## The top five

### 1. How would we know if a machine is conscious? (AI and mind)
- **Working titles:** "The Test No Machine Can Pass" / "How Would We Know?" / "The Machine That Fooled Its Maker".
- **Story per chapter (leads):** Turing's imitation game (1950); Weizenbaum's ELIZA (1966) and the secretary who asked him to leave the room; Searle's Chinese Room (1980); the Google engineer who said LaMDA was sentient (2022, his public statements only); close on Nagel's bat: we can't even prove it for each other.
- **Hero object:** a glowing chat window with a face reflected in the screen.
- **Peg:** Microsoft AI chief's warning (2 Nov), *Nature* spotlight (4 Nov), *Quanta* (4 Nov), NYU talk on AI minds (12 Nov). The debate will keep producing headlines.
- **Evidence:** "will ai become conscious" draws 365k views/yr in the main region, 119-149k median across the five; "what is consciousness" 187k/yr; Emergence's AI outliers at 49.5x and 12.6x; the r/philosophy thread above; r/askphilosophy keeps asking "How will we actually know when AI becomes conscious?"
- **Countries:** all five (the region medians are flat, so demand is global).
- **Shape:** a debate question plus a news hook.
- **Risk:** close to Emergence's own lane. Ours must be the history of *the test* told through people, not how models work inside. Stay even-handed: say what each side claims and what no one knows. No claims about any company's model being or not being conscious.

### 2. The year we lost 11 days (science history)
- **Working titles:** "The Year We Deleted 11 Days" / "Why Your Calendar Is Wrong".
- **Stories:** Caesar's 445-day "year of confusion" (46 BC); Pope Gregory's reform of 1582 (10 days cut in Catholic Europe); Britain and its colonies jump from 2 to 14 September 1752; Russia's switch in 1918 (why the "October" Revolution happened in November). Coda: Newton was born on Christmas Day 1642, or on 4 January 1643, depending on the calendar.
- **Hero object:** a wall calendar torn from the 2nd straight to the 14th.
- **Peg:** New Year, and Newton's birthday on 25 Dec. Fixed dates, 6-7 weeks out, so it can be planned now.
- **Evidence:** "why do we have 7 days in a week" 547k US median; "history of the calendar" 112-136k; a small channel (78k subs) got 746k with "The 13th Month They Deleted"; the autocomplete has "history of calendar months".
- **Countries:** UK, US, Ireland, Canada, Australia (1752 hit them all), India (dates, almanacs, many calendars in use), Europe (1582).
- **Risk:** the "riots over lost days" in 1752 are largely a myth, so it makes a good misconception beat, but it must be sourced.

### 3. What makes a painting priceless? (art and money)
- **Working titles:** "How a Painting Becomes Priceless" / "The Theft That Made the Mona Lisa".
- **Stories:** the 1911 theft of the Mona Lisa (crowds came to see the empty wall); Jo van Gogh-Bonger, the sister-in-law who made Vincent famous after his death; the Guggenheim's deaccession of a painting it sold for $62,000 that may now fetch $80m (CNN, 2 Nov); the 18 Nov Sotheby's Van Gogh sale.
- **Hero object:** the empty wall with four hooks in the Louvre, 1911.
- **Peg:** the November marquee sales (Sotheby's 18 Nov; Christie's and Phillips 17 Nov); National Gallery "Van Eyck: The Portraits" opens 21 Nov. The auction results will be news on 18-19 Nov; the video can't make that date, but it can cite the result.
- **Evidence:** "why is the mona lisa so famous" 1.4M US / 1.7M CA median, 289k/yr (TED-Ed holds the top spot); "what makes art good" has 3 small-channel wins; Great Art Explained's Last Supper at 5.1x.
- **Countries:** all five plus Europe.
- **Risk:** prices are only estimates until the sales happen; never advise anyone to buy art.

### 4. Every new technology was going to rot our brains (technology and history)
- **Working titles:** "Socrates Hated Writing" / "The Monk Who Printed a Book Against Printing".
- **Stories:** Socrates in the *Phaedrus* (writing will ruin memory); Trithemius's *In Praise of Scribes* (1492), which he then had printed; the 18th-century panic about novels; the calculator protests. The end asks: is AI different this time? Answer it fairly, both sides.
- **Hero object:** a monk's handwritten page next to a printed copy of the same page.
- **Peg:** the "AI is like the printing press" argument runs every week (r/antiai rebuttal threads; AskHistorians' "How did monks... react to the printing press?" was in its 16 Oct round-up).
- **Countries:** all.
- **Gap:** strong. Few illustrated, sourced, story-led versions.

### 5. Why does a piece of paper buy anything? (money)
- **Stories:** Song-dynasty *jiaozi*; Marco Polo amazed by Kublai Khan's paper money; John Law's Mississippi Bubble (1720); Britain's 1797-1821 suspension of gold convertibility (asked on AskHistorians in October).
- **Hero object:** a single banknote held up to the light.
- **Evidence:** "what is money actually" 327k/yr with 2 small-channel wins (95k-sub channel at 222k; 41k-sub channel at 299k).
- **Peg:** weak (Fed 9 Dec). It keeps the paused money lane alive without a series.

## One bold bet: "Close your eyes and picture an apple"
Aphantasia: some people see nothing at all when they imagine. The video opens by asking the viewer to do the test, so every viewer takes part in the first ten seconds. Stories: Francis Galton's breakfast-table survey (1880); the man who lost his mind's eye after surgery and the neurologist Adam Zeman, who named the condition (2015); Pixar's Ed Catmull finding out that he has it. Hero object: the apple (red, or blank). Demand is live: Reddit is full of "TIL I have aphantasia at 38/40/50" posts, an AMA ran 4 days ago, and *Nature* links "many people have no mental imagery" this week. It is a bet because the seed wasn't measured yet (added to the watchlist) and the story supply is thinner. Health caution: describe the research, never diagnose.
