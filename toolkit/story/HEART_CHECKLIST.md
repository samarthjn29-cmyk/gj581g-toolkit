# Heart checklist: make it personal (Sam, 26 Nov 2026)

> "Going forward make the script personal for the viewer. Like they should care about the video on a personal
> level. It should be like I have stirred an emotion in them."

The viewer should leave having *felt* something about their own life, not only having learned something.
Proved on WCL-E01 act 1: the "your screen" line (a phone screen up close: red, green, blue, "not one of them is
pink") turned a 1792 story into something happening to the viewer's own eyes right now.

## Write it in (at the outline, before drafting)

1. **One feeling per chapter, named in the outline.** Wonder, unease, tenderness, pride, loss, unfairness,
   relief, awe. Write where it peaks (the line id) and what earns it. No two chapters in a row with the same
   feeling; the video's strongest one sits at the 60-70% peak.
2. **A personal anchor per chapter.** Something the viewer owns, does or has felt, put in their hands: their
   screen, their eyes, the colour of their front door, a fruit in their kitchen, the moment they argued about a
   colour. It is said in the second person ("Look at your screen.") and comes back at least once. One anchor may
   run through the whole video as its spine (the viewer's own eyes, for *Where Colour Lives*).
3. **The people in the stories are people.** Each named person gets what they wanted, what it cost them and one
   human detail (a habit, a fear, a line they wrote). A date and a discovery are not a person.
4. **Stakes for the viewer, said once per chapter.** Why this changes how they see something they will see
   tonight. Concrete, never "this will change your life".
5. **A felt moment, not only a fact.** At least one beat per chapter where the narration slows down and lets the
   feeling land: a pause (`...`), a short line, a question to the viewer, then silence or music, not the next fact.
6. **The ending lands on the viewer.** The keeper (rule or dare) is about their own life, and the last image
   returns to the personal anchor.

## Honesty (unchanged)

- Emotion never invents a fact, a stake or an urgency. Every "you" line that states something about the body,
  the eye or the world goes through the fact-check like any other line.
- No guilt, fear-mongering or manipulation. The feeling must be one the true story earns.
- No melodrama words doing the work ("heartbreaking", "you won't believe"). Show the moment; let the viewer feel it.

## The check (Fresher test item 7, before lock)

A separate reviewer agent, which hasn't seen the research or the outline, reads the script as a viewer. Per
chapter it writes:

| Chapter | What I felt (one word) | Where (line id) | What of mine it touched | Did I care? (yes / a bit / no) | Fix |

Any chapter with "no", a felt word different from the outline's plan, or no personal anchor is a FIX with a
rewrite suggestion. The table goes in the script review file and to Sam with the hooks table.
