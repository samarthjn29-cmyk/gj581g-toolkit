# GJ 581g: scene quality, pacing, assets and render checks

Every rule here comes from Sam's clip-by-clip review of LongChat (1-2 Nov 2026), the Raise pilot (2 Nov 2026) and Raise_v1 clips A-D (3 Nov 2026). Follow it alongside every `decked-out-*` skill (and their reference files) and `gj581g-voice-music-montage`; where they disagree on drawing, assets, layout, pacing, captions or thumbnails, this skill wins. It supersedes the "something new every 3-5 s", "a visual event at least every 8 seconds" and "gaps over 5 s" checks and any unlimited caption phrase chunks in older text (PowerPoint decks keep their own 3-5 s build rhythm in `decked-out-deck-builder`).

## 0. Our own design, never a copy (Sam, 2 Nov 2026)
- Library styles and reference videos teach techniques; they are never a look to reproduce. Each series gets its own design elements: background, tile style, type, motion, sound set and characters. Claude decides the visual direction as creative director (Sam's standing instruction) and says in a line what was chosen and why; for a new series, show 2-3 original identity directions as stills of the same moment, then pilot the chosen one.
- "The Dollar Yardstick" (money series, from Nov 2026) uses **S8 Banknote**: engraved guilloché frames, wavy security-print ground, denomination-style numbers (Fraunces), Oswald caps labels, palette green #24493A / oxblood #7A2333 / gold #B98B2E on #EFE9DA; pay-slip receipts as the second device for one person's raise. Kit: `toolkit/remotion/templates/s8_banknote` (ground, `NoteFrame`, guilloché `Portrait`, graded footage `Window`, `Stamp` with an optional paper background, `Typed`, `T`, `Captions`, cue helpers `t0`/`tw` on aligned word times) and `toolkit/remotion/kits/folk_banknote/` (Folk characters, identity stills).
- **Footage and photos inside the identity:** play clips in the note's window with an engraved line-screen treatment and a slow drift; push in through the window for full-screen moments (graded to the palette), then pull back out. Photos get the same engraved two-tone treatment or are cut out onto the paper. Honesty labels and the literal-montage rule still apply. Keep the grade light enough to read the subject; use the sharp part of the clip.

## 1. Where images come from (in this order)
1. **Vectors we draw or take from free libraries.** Sam asked for free illustration libraries to be used "more often and freely" and allowed vectors (1 Nov 2026). Use Open Peeps (CC0; the `react-peeps` package, two layers so shirt and skin take different colours), Open Doodles, Humaaans, unDraw, and icon sets such as Lucide, Tabler, Phosphor and OpenMoji. Install through npm or GitHub (the workspace blocks most websites), recolour to the palette, and log each one in `media/licence_log.csv` with its credit.
2. **Free stock photos and footage, wherever they fit** (Sam, 2 Nov 2026: "use stock images wherever you can"): Unsplash (connector), free Adobe Stock, Mixkit (Free License items via `toolkit/media/mixkit.py`, which refuses restricted ones), Pexels and Pixabay (downloaded on the laptop), public-domain archives. Logged and credited as the media skill says.
3. **AI-generated images only as a last resort.** Gemini and Google Flow work through the built-in browser on Sam's laptop (Claude writes the prompt, generates, downloads from `C:\Users\samar\Downloads`, files it in `media\ai_images`). But both stamp a visible sparkle watermark that Sam does not want, and the switch to turn it off is not available for his account in India. Never crop, cover or paint out that mark. Use AI images only if Sam asks for one or Google's own setting becomes available; then label them "AI-generated illustration", never as evidence, no real people, logos or known characters, and turn on YouTube's synthetic-content disclosure.
4. Clear any test downloads after use only with Sam's permission (the shell cannot delete files on his laptop; ask him or use the delete-permission tool when it exists).

## 2. Drawing quality
- **Characters with volume**, not stick figures, with face changes cued to the narration.
  - **Money series and later: the Folk kit** (`kits/folk_banknote/folk.jsx` for busts; `kits/folk_banknote/rig.jsx`, the full-body rig with walk, gestures and lip sync, see `gj581g-character-story`), our own flat-vector characters drawn from scratch: clean shapes, no outlines, no wobble, one shade tone per colour; 15 expressions (the 8 originals plus the Heckler's moods: impatient, pushy, deflated, exasperated, groan, envious, warm), eye types including `roll` and `shut`; hair (bob, bun, curly, short), tops, glasses, hoop earrings and skin tones; a card-holding pose. Sam asked for "extremely neat and well drawn" characters that differ from earlier videos. Extend it (sitting at a desk, hands holding two props, new hair) rather than switching libraries; check every new pose at full size and at phone size before use.
  - **The cast of The Dollar Yardstick** (as drawn in video 1, the reference for every later video, thumbnail and still): Barbara (curly near-black hair, deep-brown skin #8D5B3E, orange-red sweater #C4623F), Ann (brown bob, light skin #F1C7A4, slate-blue blazer #2E4A5E); import these from the video's own constants (`ANN`, `BARB` in the scene code) instead of retyping them: the thumbnail kit had its own Barbara (light skin, oxblood blazer) until 3 Nov. Cousin Sam (from video 2; test look: short black hair, skin #A86F4C, banknote-green top). Then the Heckler (from video 2: hair in a bun (Sam, 4 Nov 2026; it was a bob, like Ann's), mustard-gold jumper, gold hoops; `kits/folk_banknote/heckler.jsx` has her look `HECK`, her mood poses and the mood sheet). Each new character must be told apart from the others at phone size by hair and top colour (check the cast lineup still, `kits/folk_banknote/lineup.jsx`, before adding anyone).
  - Earlier videos (Tokens, LongChat) used Open Peeps; don't reuse it in the money series. If using Open Peeps busts elsewhere, use the `Shirt` body: `Explaining`, `PointingUp` and similar bodies carry their own patterned shirt and ignore the palette.
- **Designing a new recurring character** (Sam, 3 Nov 2026): first a sheet of at least 10 designs in clearly different drawing manners (series style, stick figure, 8-bit pixel, 1930s rubber-hose, engraved portrait, blob, paper cut-out, felt puppet, geometric, ink sketch, mid-century, silhouette...) so Sam picks the manner; then the pick drawn properly in its moods (one panel per mood with a sample line); then the character in a real scene mock. Sam picked the Folk style (design 1) for the Heckler.
- **The Heckler on screen:** she pops up from the lower-left edge (upper body, large enough that her mouth reads on a phone), says her line with Rhubarb lip sync and the face and pose of the line's `mood` tag, then ducks back down. She never covers a chart, a number, the captions or the empty bottom-right corner. Her lines are never shown as text (section 4).
- **Scenes with a setting** (wall, window, shelf, desk, mug, plant) instead of objects floating on paper; props as paper with a lifted shadow and a folded corner, sticky notes with tape, gauges with a bezel, ticks and numbers.
- **Straight things have straight edges.** Roofs, spires, boxes, frames and furniture use the straight-edged `poly` helper with small wobble (0.3-0.8). The rounded `wob` curve is only for organic shapes (plants, steam, hills). A roof drawn with `wob` comes out as a dome (Sam's note on clip D).
- **No scribbled emphasis.** Wavy red "scribble" strokes across data or a chat look weird (Sam's note on clip C). Show the point by changing the data itself (dots turning red, a bar growing), with a clean straight strike, a tidy bracket or a stamp.
- **Accent colour must suit the character's skin tone.** Teal clashed with the face and was replaced by denim blue `#35609E` (Espresso & Denim). Before locking a palette, render the character next to the accent.
- **Readable comparisons.** A claim like "answers about a third longer" gets two labelled bars inside clear space, shown before any cross-out lands, not a small shape under the cross (Sam's note on clip D). Bars are tall enough to compare at phone size (the Raise three-countries chart doubled its bar scale).

## 3. Pacing (from 2 Nov 2026; Reddit lesson "AI is great at volume, bad at taste: boring edits and too many sound effects", adopted by Sam)
- Every storyboard line carries **`treatment`**, one of: character, document, chart, number, map, typography, evidence-footage, montage, archive, photo, motion-graphic, title; and **`changes`**: the words in that line where the picture visibly changes (a number lands, a card appears, a cut; `["word", k]` for the k-th occurrence of a repeated word).
- **`toolkit/story/pace_lint.py storyboard.json [timing.json] [sfx_cues.json]` must pass** before any render (`build.py` runs it after the mix): a picture change at least every **6 s** (warning above 4.5 s), no run of one treatment over **20 s**, no montage over 20 s without evidence, sound effects **at most 8 per minute** and at least 1.5 s apart. Before voicing it estimates timing at 2.6 words/s (animatic stage); after voicing it uses the aligned word times. The Heckler popping up counts as a picture change.
- Moving footage may hold 4.5-6 s (a warning, not a failure); an empty card never may: the label or first number appears as the line starts (the Raise UK card sat empty for 1.5 s).
- **No empty note while the narrator speaks.** When one picture (a `Show`) ends and the next starts on a later word, the note sits blank in between: keep the last picture up until the next one arrives, or bring the next element in on the line's first word (Raise caught two such gaps of about 2 s: before "Let's call that a Barbara raise" and before the short answer).
- A try-it window counts as a picture change only if its countdown or picture visibly moves.
- **Readable for at least 1.5 s** (Sam on video 2's "BY A MILE", 4 Nov 2026: it "should come clearly"; it was up for under a second): every stamp, label and number stays readable for 1.5 s or more. Nothing readable lands in the last 1.5 s before a scene change: bring it in earlier in the line (on its first words, not its last), or hold it into the next scene. A takeaway card shows each of its lines within about a second of the card appearing, not on the line's own word. `av_check.py` flags text readable for under 1.5 s (above the caption band, away from the clip's first and last 0.6 s); the last-1.5-s and takeaway rules are checked with stills of each scene's final second.
- **A long line still needs beats:** when one narration line runs over about 6 s, put a visual beat on a key word (a pulse on the number, a tag dropping, a coin shrinking); `av_check.py` finds stills of 6 s or more that the storyboard's `changes` missed (video 2 had four, 6-9 s each).
- **Numbers on screen vs spoken:** charts may show the figures; the narration speaks only the key one (`gj581g-first-lecture` rule 1), so a chart's other numbers need a picture change (a bar moving, a label landing) rather than a spoken read-out.

## 4. Captions
- Burnt-in on every scene (word cards included), **at most 8 words on screen at a time**, current word highlighted, timed on the aligned word times (in the Kokoro animatic, estimated within each line); a long line steps through in 8-word pages (the Raise retests line ran off both edges of the frame). Dark pill at y >= 990; content stays above about y 980 so the captions never cover it.
- Captions show the words as written for the viewer (the line's `text`), not the clone's `say` respelling.
- **The one exception: the Heckler's lines are never shown** (Sam, 3 Nov 2026): no burnt-in caption and no speech bubble while she speaks; the caption pill is empty during her line. Her lip sync and face carry it, and the narrator's next line must make sense without her for muted viewers. `timing_srt.py` and the S8 `Captions` component skip her lines (`who: "heckler"`), so she is never in the burnt-in captions or the YouTube subtitle file.

## 5. Layout rules (check every scene)
- **Nothing leaves the frame:** labels stay at least 40 px inside the edges, and text never runs past its card.
- **Readable on a phone:** any text the viewer must read is at least 24 px at 1080p (chart legends at 20 px and a 20-24 px box of monthly figures were unreadable in Raise and had to be enlarged); source lines are the one exception (about 20 px, below the note).
- **Centred or right-aligned text that also animates:** the alignment transform and the motion transform must be composed (S8 `T` does this); otherwise the motion overwrites the alignment and titles and values slide off their cards (Raise pilot v1).
- **No overlaps:** a label never touches a dot, a bracket, a chimney, a stamp or another label. Place labels where the drawing will be at its most complete, not where it is at the first frame (the chart builds, the house grows wings). Decorative rosettes sit beside a title, not behind its letters. Year labels sit above a note frame's border band, not on it. A stamp and the box next to it are checked at their final positions (the Raise "Barbara raise" stamp covered a by-month box until it was moved).
- **A reaction stamp sits beside the number it comments on, never on top of it**, on a paper background, with a short arrow if needed (video 2's "BY A MILE" first covered Sam's "+9%").
- **Text sits next to what it names,** with a short arrow if needed ("remember this guess?" under the house with an arrow to the door note).
- **Contrast:** text on a mid-tone surface (desk wood, footage) uses the dark ink or the negative red, never the orange callout (Sam's note on clip E). Light text only on dark fills. A stamp over footage gets a paper background.
- **YouTube end screen:** the last scene leaves the top right empty for the element it points to.
- **A pop-up character's zone:** in scenes where the Heckler can appear, keep the lower-left third (about x < 520) free of charts and labels.

## 6. Technical traps (Remotion)
- **HTML text (`Hand`, `Mono`, `Stamp`, `Hero`, `Label`, `Src`, S8 `T`) never goes inside an SVG `<Sheet>` or `<svg>`, and SVG pieces (`Ink`, `Grid`, `Paper`, `Sticky`, `NoteFrame`, `Portrait`, `rect`, `path`...) never go outside one.** Either way they silently don't render (the missing "11 of 13" squares chart, the blank sticky-note labels). `toolkit/story/scene_lint.py scenes.jsx` catches both.
- **Cue words spoken more than once need an index.** `cue(n, 'word')` fires on the first occurrence, so a repeated word cues the visual too early (the "Hold that thought" box). Write `cue(n, 'word', k)` (or S8 `tw(id, 'word', k)`) with k explicit, even when 0. `toolkit/story/cue_lint.py scenes.jsx story/slides.json` flags repeats and missing words.
- **Cue on aligned word times, never on estimates** in final clips: after clone voicing, `timing.json` carries per-word times and every cue, caption and sound effect follows them (estimated cues landed up to a second late in Raise v1). Only the Kokoro animatic runs on estimated word times. When a line is reworded, update the cue words in the scene code in the same change ("roughly flat" -> "about the same" moved the cue from 'flat' to 'same').
- **A comment never goes inside a line of code that continues** (a `// note` placed before `const t9 = ...` on the same line commented the declaration out and the render failed with "t9 is not defined"): put the comment at the end of the line.
- **Paint order:** an element drawn later covers an earlier one. A window or card that appears later than a moving item must be drawn before it in the code (the consolidated summary hidden under the new chat window).
- **The lints run before every render:** `build.py` stops on a pace-lint failure; run `scene_lint.py` and `cue_lint.py` on the scene code yourself before each build, since the build does not run them.
- **Never voice and render at the same time:** the voice clone and a render together run out of memory (exit 137). Queue them; never run two renders at once.
- **Edits apply at the next render's start.** If a render is queued, an edit made now lands in it; check which clips need re-rendering after each fix and queue them one at a time.
- **Music across clips (when there is music):** the mix keeps one fixed gain per track and global offsets, and drops a music cue that has already been replaced before the clip starts (otherwise two tracks overlap).
- **Composition ids** may not contain an underscore (Remotion rejects "Raise_TS22"; use "RaiseTS22").

## 7. Review before sending a clip
1. Run the lints (the build runs pace; run scene and cue lints on the scene code).
2. Look at `build/frames_sheet.png` (a frame at the end of each line), plus stills at the **start** of every line in new scenes (no empty note while the narrator speaks, section 3), and at stills of the busiest moment of every new or changed scene: frame edges, overlaps at final positions, contrast, the character, straight edges, captions, text under 24 px, anything unreadable at phone size.
3. Pull extra frames at the moments the narration names (stamps, numbers landing, footage, the Heckler's pop-ups) and look again before sending.
3b. Run `toolkit/build/av_check.py` on the laptop copy (or the build's `avcheck` stage): fix hard faults first; confirm each review item with a still before acting on it.
4. Send the laptop copy (under 30 MB; capped by bitrate, see `gj581g-producer` 6b), say in one line what changed, and save it to `projects\<Topic>_v<N>\` on G:.
5. When Sam flags something, check the same kind of problem in every other scene and fix them together (one scribble note led to three fixes), and add a line to `toolkit/LESSONS.md`.

## 8. Thumbnails (v2, Sam, 3 Nov 2026)
Thumbnails are no longer stills of the video's flat scenes: those blend into the feed and read as clutter at phone size (compared side by side in the 3 Nov dark feed mock). Build them from the four house templates in `toolkit/thumbs/templates_v2/` (A editorial, B hero object, C split, D big number + 3D; details in `decked-out-youtube-packaging` section 2) and the style repository `styles/THUMBNAIL_LIBRARY.md` (TS01-TS28). **Mix and merge freely** (Sam, 3 Nov 2026): templates are starting points; colours, sizes, pictures, styles and graphs can be combined into something new.
- **One hero object with depth:** a real object from a free stock photo (on white or cut out, with its shadow) or our own 3D render (three.js, `coins.html` as the model: metal, glass, paper or clay materials, environment map, soft shadow), or a bold poster symbol (TS22). The video's characters may appear small as a secondary element, never as the whole picture. (Raise_v1's set leaned on the characters in A, B and D; from video 2 on, at least three of the four lead with an object or symbol.)
- **2-4 words** that add the stakes or the question to the title, in a heavy grotesk or the series display face at 90 px cap height or more at 1280 width; one accent device (torn banner, yellow label, accent bar, hand-written note). No swear words on thumbnails.
- **Series tag** (PW-06, Sam, 3 Nov 2026): every Dollar Yardstick thumbnail carries a small "The Dollar Yardstick · No. N" tag with the ruler icon, clear of the bottom-right badge zone and legible at 380 px; other series get their own tag the same way.
- **The video's identity** through its palette roles, display face and series device; a clean or neutral ground or one photo; no logos, brands, recognisable faces or other creators' frames.
- Layout: one focal point, the bottom-right 140x60 px clear for the duration badge, nothing important within 40 px of the edges; symmetric shapes are drawn symmetric (the TS22 eye's iris sat off-centre because the eye path wasn't).
- Check each at full size, in the dark feed mock (`templates_v2/feed.html`, 380 px wide with title and badge) and at about 170 px wide, and make sure the four look clearly different from each other before sending.
- **Test & compare runs the thumbnails under one title**, so the three sent there must all pair with that title: a thumbnail that repeats a word of the title (Raise D, "RAISE?") stays out. Characters on thumbnails are the video's own designs (see the cast above).
