That's a treadmill.") may stay a little brisk if every word is clear; say so to Sam when sending.

When a word keeps failing during voicing, reword the line straight away rather than spending all 8 tries, and update the script, the fact-check CSV and the clip scripts together.

## 1. The humour voice (research-story, every script)
- The narration is Sam's own cloned voice, so the humour is **his**: dry asides, deadpan understatement, self-aware jokes about the video itself, a line that admits the obvious. Light, never smug, no stereotypes, no invented personal history. Real public figures and institutions can be the target (move 9); viewers, members of the public and the video's own characters are teased kindly, never mocked.
- **Joke moves that work in the clone** (from R08/R09/R11; use several kinds per video, not one):
  1. **Literal-minded follow-through** (R11): take a phrase at its word until it breaks ("surely the top longevity expert should be about 130, and unavailable for comment"). It can be aimed at a public figure's own words (move 9).
  2. **Act-out of the finding** (R11): perform the confused participant so the joke *is* the result ("How does a zip work? Well, it's got the teeth and the... they're teeth."). Keep it to one or two lines and mark the energy shift in the storyboard.
  3. **Narrator as subject** (R11): the narrator admits he'd fail the test too, or becomes the experiment ("I love octagons, me. Why? No idea."). Never a made-up anecdote about his life.
  4. **Bathos list** (R11): a triplet that escalates and lands on the mundane ("your mate's podcast about carp fishing is fine... two blokes in a shed talking football for two hours... that's not a podcast, that's a cry for help").
  5. **Objection and answer** (R09): voice the viewer's scoff, then answer it with the next example. When the Heckler is in the video, she can voice the scoff.
  6. **Staged gag in footage** (R09): show the thing the aside names for 2-4 s, then back to the point (allowed by the montage rule below, and only when the clip really shows that thing).
  7. **The admission** (R08): say the obvious thing out loud ("didn't someone already explain this? yes").
  8. **Swearing** (Sam, 3 Nov 2026: "You can include swearing", replacing the 2 Nov near-swears-only rule). Real swear words are allowed in the narration and the narrator's captions (uncensored). Use them where they land a line, usually as the last word of a calm, deadpan build-up (British toolkit B29), and occasionally, so they keep their punch. Near-swears and cut-offs ("what in the actual... Right.") are still good tools. Rules:
     - Never in the title, thumbnail, description or tags: YouTube still limits ads for profanity there.
     - No dense or constant strong swearing: frequent use can still limit ads.
     - Strong words in the opening seconds are no longer penalised (YouTube, July 2025).
     - Never slurs or their sound-alikes. No swearing at viewers or at a group of people.
     - Never inside numbers, claims, caveats or quotes.
     - Clone check: make each swear word a `must` phrase, so the word check confirms it was said cleanly.
  9. **Mocking real people** (Sam, 3 Nov 2026). Public figures, institutions and companies can be the butt of the joke for what they publicly said or did: politicians, central bankers, finance ministers, CEOs, pundits, the video's own field. The best version quotes their real words and follows them through literally (move 1; toolkit B30). Rules:
     - Every factual statement about them is true and sourced, and goes in the fact-check CSV like any claim.
     - Quotes are verbatim and dated.
     - No made-up quote or scene presented as real; a parody voice is labelled as parody on screen.
     - Jokes about conduct and ideas, never about race, religion, gender, sexuality, disability or appearance tied to those.
     - Never private individuals, members of the public or commenters.
     - Balance: across a video, aim at whoever earns it, not one side of politics (channel balance rule).
     - Use footage or photos of a real person only when the media rules allow it (free, licensed or public domain).
     - Never fake their voice: no voice clone of a real person.
- **Where jokes go:** between facts, at the end of a build-up, right after a big number has landed, in the viewer-voice lines, and in the chapter hand-offs. About one real laugh line per chapter and a few smaller asides.
- **Where jokes never go:** inside a number, a claim, a caveat, a source line or a quote; in the fact-check rows; in the final answer. When the argument turns serious (the twist, the fairness turn, the verdict), the jokes stop, as R08 and R11 both do in their last minutes.
- The joke is spoken, not captioned as a gag: captions show the line's `text`; no emoji, no "(laughs)".
- Mark humour lines in the storyboard's `energy` field ("dry") so the delivery stays flat; write deadpan lines with full stops.

## 1b. Charm and the punch-up pass (Sam, 3 Nov 2026: "increase the humour and charm in our script")
Humour is planned, not left to luck. These add to section 1 and keep all of its limits (never inside a fact, real people only within move 9, jokes stop at the answer).
- **Characters with personalities carry the comedy.** Give each recurring character one clear trait (Barbara: cheerfully overconfident, already pricing a jet ski; Ann: dry, quietly smug; the Heckler: blunt and impatient, secretly won over). A plain fact becomes funny because of who it happens to. Show the reaction with the Folk rig (`gj581g-character-story`) instead of describing it.
- **Picture against voice.** The cheapest reliable laugh in our format: the narration stays deadpan and accurate while the picture contradicts or exaggerates it ("Barbara is thrilled" over a receipt that keeps printing). Picture gags need no re-voicing, but they still go only into clips that are not yet approved: approved clips and joined videos are frozen (Sam, 3 Nov 2026).
- **Personify the abstract force** (inflation as a polite thief who takes one biscuit from every packet; a raise as a balloon with a slow leak). One personification per video, carried through.
- **A running gag with a payoff:** plant an object or wish early (Barbara's jet-ski fund jar), let it reappear unchanged in every scene, and pay it off near the end (the jet-ski key ring). Callbacks reward viewers who stay.
- **Oddly specific details** are funnier than general ones ("the price of bread you remember from school"). A joke never adds a number; use the video's own fact-checked figures or its hypothetical.
- **Meta, once or twice a video:** a self-aware line about the video itself ("This is the part where an economics video shows you a chart.").
- **Warmth is the charm:** the narrator is on the viewer's side ("the illusion is just very good at its job"), hands them the tool ("your own payslip is the best source of all"), and treats the characters kindly (warm, not mocked).
- **Comic timing without silence:** the beat before a punchline is a picture change (a cut, a character's face, a 2-3 s cutaway), never a silent hold. A sound sting only rarely (inside the sound budget).
- **Global audience:** jokes about universal things (payday, shopping, optimism, family), not local references.
- **British humour (from Dollar Yardstick video 2):** understatement, the slightly put-upon narrator, the polite inanimate villain, bathos lists, phrase ladders, interruptions (the Heckler), describing the format while doing it, and a sincere landing. The pattern bank B01-B30, with sample lines and clone notes, is in `styles/BRITISH_HUMOUR_TOOLKIT.md`. Pick 6-10 patterns per video and tag each joke with its B-ID in the storyboard.
- **The punch-up pass (a workflow step):** after the facts are locked and before the first line is voiced, a separate pass marks the joke slots (after big numbers, chapter hand-offs, character lines, the running gag, the Heckler's interjections), writes about three options per slot, keeps the best one, and sends Sam a numbered punch-up file (`<Topic>_punchup.md`, IDs P1, P2...) in two tiers: 1 picture only (no re-voice), 2 new lines; both go only into clips not yet approved (the old tier 3, inserts into approved clips, is retired: Sam, 3 Nov 2026, "all changes will apply to the next video"). Do the pass before the first line is voiced, so its picks reach every clip; picks that arrive after a clip's approval go to the next video. Sam approves by ID. Dry lines end with full stops and carry `energy: "dry"`; test any three-beat punchline in the clone before the scene is voiced. First used on Raise_v1 (`projects\Raise_v1\story\Raise_v1_punchup.md`; its picks arrived after the video was approved, so they move to video 2).

## 2. Talked delivery (research-story writes it, the clone voices it)
The aim is narration that sounds **talked, not read**, while the voice itself stays at its natural pace (never sped up). These are tools, not quotas: Sam asked for **no hard limits** on questions or tags (1 Nov 2026). Use them when they are needed and the context allows.
- **Questions the narrator answers himself.** R11 asks one roughly every half minute; that describes his style, it is **not a target**. Use one where it moves the thought forward or gives the viewer a beat to guess: "That's not a podcast, is it? That's a cry for help." / "So is thinking free? Not quite." Skip them where a plain statement lands harder, in evidence and numbers, and at the serious turn. The chapter titles stay questions as before.
- **Conversational tags** ("right?" after a claim the viewer can check in their head, "you know" before an everyday example, "like" before an illustration, "I mean" to restate, "actually" to correct an assumption). **No target count and no ceiling:** use them where the line sounds stiff without one and the context allows it; leave them out where they would blur a fact or sound put on in the clone. Never stack them, and read the line aloud in your head: if the tag adds nothing, cut it.
- **Never inside facts:** no tag inside a number, a quote, a source line or a caveat ("about, you know, 30 percent" is banned); the fact-check text must stay identical to the narration around the number.
- **Clear sentences** (Sam, 1 Nov 2026; length targets since 3 Nov in `gj581g-first-lecture` rule 10: average 10-15 words, nothing over about 25): build each sentence so it is easy to understand the first time it is heard, and say each idea in plain, precise words. Within those targets, length follows the idea: one clear thought per sentence where possible, the subject and verb early, no stacked clauses that make the listener hold three things at once, and no jargon without a plain-word explanation. Read it aloud in your head; if you have to read it twice, rewrite it.
- **Pace that follows the content, written in, not sped up:** set-ups of an experiment and the ending slower, lists and rants quicker, through the wording and punctuation. Never change the tempo of the narrator's voice itself.
- **No silent holds** (Sam, 1 Nov 2026): no dead-air holds after the twist, before the answer or anywhere else. Only the natural gaps between sentences stay. Big moments land through the words and the visual. Storyboard `hold` is always 0. A try-it window (a visible countdown with the picture moving, like the Ann-and-Barbara test) is not a silent hold and may run its length. **Exception:** a video built as a video essay (S7 montage essay as the base style) may keep one or two holds, over music or picture.
- **Turn the argument on itself** once, just before the answer (R11): tell the viewer not to take the video's word for it and how they could check (the sources are in the description).
- **Tags in the clone:** "right?" and "you know" can come out swallowed; the word check catches it; treat a missing tag as a re-roll only if the line reads wrong without it.
- **Clip labels when commenting on other media** (R11): show, date, segment type and title on screen for every platform clip, within the commentary-clip rule (10-15 s, never b-roll).

## 3. Music (changed 2 Nov 2026)
- **The money series ("The Dollar Yardstick") pilot has no music.** Sam: "Investigations" (96 bpm) wasn't urgent; "Hitman" (152 bpm) was "way too different from our topic and doesn't suit it". Any later music must suit the topic, and Sam chooses it.
- **When:** the sampler comes after the script is locked (it is heard under the real narration); music cues are added to the storyboard only after Sam picks.
- **How music is chosen:** shortlist 3-4 openly licensed tracks that suit the topic (CC0, CC BY 4.0, or music Sam owns; Kevin MacLeod at incompetech.com downloads directly from the workspace: `https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/mp3-royaltyfree/<Title>.mp3`; Free Music Archive; Pixabay Music and the YouTube Audio Library when Sam downloads them); measure tempo; build a sampler with 12-15 s of each **under the narration at the real mix level**; send it; use only the track Sam picks. Never place music Sam hasn't heard. Never a track that needs attribution we can't give, never a commercial track, never AI-generated music presented as human.
- **Mix rules (when there is music):** a music bed of about -24 LUFS, sidechain-ducked by a further 4 dB or more while Sam speaks, so it sits at least 12 dB under the -16 LUFS voice, fades of 1-2 s at cue changes; one fixed gain per track and global offsets so levels match and the music runs on across clip joins; a cue already replaced before a clip starts is dropped for that clip. The `build.py` mix step (or the project's `mix.py`, called with `none` for no music) does this; after adding or changing music, re-run `build.py --from mix`.
- Log every track in `media/licence_log.csv` (title, artist, licence, URL, credit line) and credit it in the description ("Music: <title> by <artist>, CC BY 4.0, <url>").

## 4. Sound effects (allowed since 2 Nov 2026; budget added the same day)
- **Sound effects are allowed.** Each video or series gets a small themed set that belongs to its look. For the money series (S8 Banknote): a note flick or paper slide when a tile lands, a stamp thud, a register "ka-ching" when a key number appears, coins for small amounts, a typewriter on pay slips, a soft whoosh for push-ins through the note window. The set is synthesised in-house (`toolkit/remotion/templates/s8_banknote/sfx/make_sfx.py`), so no licence is needed; outside sources are Freesound CC0, Mixkit and Pixabay, logged with the credit line when one is required.
- **At most 8 per minute and at least 1.5 s apart** (`pace_lint.py` fails above that; the v3 pilot had 24 in 61 s, exactly the "too many sound effects" problem). Every sound is tied to something on screen at that frame; nothing plays on its own; none under a sentence the viewer must follow closely; none inside a number being read out unless it is the cue for that number landing.
- Mix: about -26 to -30 LUFS short-term, under the voice; no loud stingers.
- Jokes can use a sound (record scratch, register, sad trombone-style cue), but the joke still never sits inside a number or a claim.

## 5. Decorative montage (media plans it, video places it)
- Footage may **set mood and rhythm, not only show the literal fact**: free stock, archive and public-domain paintings cut in between the evidence shots (a Venus flytrap for a trap, dice or a roulette wheel for a gamble, Bruegel for chaos). **Gambling imagery and footage are fine** (Sam, 2 Nov 2026): dice, cards, chips, slot machines and casinos may carry a metaphor for chance or risk.
- **A clip that shows a named thing must show that thing** (Sam's note on LongChat clip C, 2 Nov 2026): when the narration names an action or object ("making snowballs"), the clip shows that action or object, or there is no clip. A near miss (falling snow for snowball-making) reads as a mistake, not as mood. If no free clip matches, keep the drawn scene instead.
- **The evidence rule still holds for evidence shots:** the number, the paper, the place and the real event are shown as themselves (snips, charts, maps, labelled footage, a dataset's own pages with the line highlighted and the source under it, as R11 does). Decorative shots never carry a number or a label that claims they are the event, and any clip that could be mistaken for the real event or study keeps its honesty label.
- **Sources:** free only, as the media skill lists them (Mixkit Free License via `toolkit/media/mixkit.py`, which refuses restricted items; Pexels and Pixabay by web download on the laptop; Unsplash; free Adobe Stock; Prelinger and other public-domain archives; NASA; Library of Congress; museum CC0 collections and Wikimedia Commons for paintings). Stock comes before AI-generated images (see `gj581g-scene-quality`). Credit paintings and CC BY clips in the description.
- **Use the sharp part of a clip:** slow it down rather than letting it play into a blurred or dark end (the Raise grocery clip).
- **Rhythm:** in a montage stretch, 2-4 s per clip, cut on the phrase; no stretch of pure montage longer than about 20 s without a fact, a chart or a quote on screen; keep the riso balance rule.
- **Grade to the identity sheet:** decorative clips get the video's tint or grain; evidence clips stay untinted where the viewer must read them.
- Record every montage clip in the media plan with its purpose ("mood: gamble"), and mark a clip dropped in review as UNUSED in the licence log.

## Checklist before voicing and before the final mix
- Script: `say` fields where the clone needs them; `must` phrases and `tight` lines marked; lines end on words that can carry the stress; names tested in the clone (new names); at least one dry line per chapter, several joke moves (swearing allowed, never in title, thumbnail or description; real people only within move 9, each claim about them in the fact-check), none inside a fact; jokes stop at the serious turn.
- Second voice (if the video has one): every heckler line tagged `who: "heckler"` and `mood`; no number or claim in her lines; the narrator's next line stands alone without hers; her takes word-checked; played at 1.2x; not in the captions or subtitles.
- Humour and charm: punch-up pass done before the first line is voiced and Sam's picks applied; each character has a trait; at least one picture-against-voice gag per chapter; one running gag planted and paid off; warmth lines; no joke adds a number.
- Talked delivery: questions and tags used where they help (no quotas), never inside a number; every sentence clear on first hearing; one turn-it-on-itself line before the answer; no silent holds (try-it windows and video essays excepted).
- Voice: fact-check passed (or pilot waiver); voice lint clean (or approved lines allowed); profile `sam_voice_v1`, scene mode, long scenes in parts, every scene passes words + similarity + must + tight + pace; odd words in kept takes double-checked with `medium.en`; every meaning-changing mishearing re-rolled or reworded; new garbles added to `hazards`; no tempo change on the narrator; no render running during voicing.
- Final file: the voice finish B applied at the mix (from the profile); the `qa` stage passed with no hard faults and its listening checklist goes to Sam with the MP3; `build.py` check stage reports "every line ends audibly"; listen to the last line of each clip before sending.
- Music: none, or Sam's pick from a sampler heard under the narration; at least 12 dB under the voice while he speaks, one gain per track, continuous across clip joins, logged and credited.
- Sound effects: themed set, each tied to an on-screen action, at most 8 per minute, under the voice, logged if not in-house.
- Montage: every decorative clip free and logged, showing exactly what the narration names, no number or event label on it, no montage stretch over about 20 s without evidence on screen.
